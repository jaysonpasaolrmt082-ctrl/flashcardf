Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the original article **"Cross-Species Transcriptomic Analysis Identifies Colorectal Cancer-Associated Molecular Pathways Induced by *Schistosoma japonicum* Infection"** for consideration in *Acta Medica Philippina*.

**The problem.** *Schistosoma japonicum* remains endemic in the Philippines, China and Indonesia, and chronic intestinal infection has long been associated with colorectal neoplasia. IARC classifies *S. japonicum* as possibly carcinogenic to humans (Group 2B), yet the molecular programs that might link the parasite-induced colonic response to colorectal carcinogenesis are poorly understood.

**What we did.** Under a pre-specified analysis plan, we integrated a transcriptomic signature from *S. japonicum*-infected mouse colon with colon (TCGA-COAD) and rectal (TCGA-READ) adenocarcinoma data, using strict one-to-one orthology, formal tests of overlap and directional concordance, Hallmark and hypothesis-driven DNA-damage-response (DDR) enrichment analysis, network prioritization, and replication.

**Why it matters for the journal's readers.** The work addresses a disease of direct public-health relevance to the Philippines, identifies candidate pathways and biomarkers for prospective validation in Filipino endemic populations, and points to DDR/PARP biology as a testable mechanistic link. We are careful throughout to present the findings as associations, not evidence of causation.
